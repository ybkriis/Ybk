# Escaping GTA 6's Wanted System: narration only

## COLD OPEN

In GTA 5, you rob a store, and the stars are on your screen before you reach the door.
In GTA 6, you can rob a store with nobody watching… and nothing happens.
In GTA 5, you lose the cops, and you can drive the same car straight past them.
In GTA 6, that car is what they're looking for.
GTA 5 tops out at five stars.
GTA 6 brings back the sixth.
And in GTA 5, the police forget you the second the stars go out.
In GTA 6, in the words of one of the people who built it…
"Our dispatch is extremely specific in what it knows."

## AUTHORITY + METHOD

GTA 6 has been out for less than a week. It's sitting around a ninety-nine on Metacritic, and the system people keep talking about isn't the map or the story. It's the cops.
So I went through what Rockstar has actually said, what the guides and previews agree on, and what's still just rumour, and lined it up against how escaping worked in GTA 5.
When something comes from Rockstar, I'll say so. When it's from guides, I'll say that too.
And there are seven systems here. Every one of them feeds the next, and by the end you'll see why the oldest trick in GTA, just outrunning the cops, barely works anymore.
There's also one level where, according to the guides, there's no escape at all. I'll get to that one last.

## CHAPTER 1 — THE WITNESS

Let's start where every wanted level starts: with someone seeing you.
In GTA 5, the police are basically psychic. If a cop sees you, stars.
Pedestrians could phone it in, sure, but most of the time the game just knew.
GTA 6 starts from the opposite assumption. According to Rob Nelson at Rockstar North, a crime has to be witnessed, or it has to set off an alarm, before it's reported to the police.
No witness. No alarm. No wanted level.
That sounds like a small change. It isn't. It means the first question in every crime is no longer "can I get away?" It's "who saw that?"
Rockstar has done this before. Nelson has pointed to Red Dead Redemption 2's witness system as the foundation for this one.
In Red Dead, you could catch a witness before they reached the law. Whether you can do that in GTA 6 hasn't been confirmed, so don't take that from me.
But here's the part that changes everything. A witness in GTA 6 doesn't just report that a crime happened.
They describe who did it.

## CHAPTER 2 — THE DESCRIPTION

This is the heat system.
Under your stars, GTA 6 shows little red icons, and each one is something the police know about you.
What you're wearing. What you're driving. What you look like. The weapon you used.
And, because there are two of you, the fact that they're looking for a couple.
Rockstar has said it directly: the police recognise your car, and they know you're a pair.
In other words, they remember your shirt.
Compare that to GTA 5, where the police knew exactly one thing about you: where you were.
Your identity didn't matter. Your car didn't matter. Lose them once and you were a stranger again.
In GTA 6, the stars tell you how hard they're coming. The icons tell you who they're coming for.
And there's a reason Rockstar put the word "specific" in that quote. A witness can be wrong. They can only see so much. They forget.
Which is why the most dangerous witness in GTA 6 isn't a person.
It's a camera.

## CHAPTER 3 — THE CAMERA

Remember that store from the start?
Nobody watching, nothing happens. Except shops in GTA 6 can have security cameras, and guides report that CCTV can record your crime.
A camera doesn't panic. It doesn't run. You can't scare it into keeping quiet.
And according to the heat-icon breakdowns, a CCTV sighting is one of the things that can show up under your stars.
You might have seen people comparing this to real-world licence-plate camera networks, things like Flock. To be clear: there's no evidence Rockstar licensed anything or partnered with anyone. It's a comparison, not a fact.
What it does mean is this: the old GTA 5 plan, kick the door, grab the cash, run,
now leaves evidence behind before you've even started running.
And once they have evidence, the stars stop working like a timer…
and start working like a status.

## CHAPTER 4 — THE STARS

In GTA 5, stars meant one thing: how many cops, how much firepower.
In GTA 6, guides consistently describe the stars changing appearance depending on what's going on.
Hollow stars: a crime's been reported, police are investigating, but they don't know it's you. Keep your head down and you can walk away.
Solid white: they've seen you. This is a chase.
Flashing: they're losing track of you. That's your window. Not to fight. To vanish.
And red: you got away from the chase, but you're still inside the area they're searching.
You'll see grey stars mentioned online too, but the guides can't agree on what grey means, so I'm not going to pretend I know.
Then there's the count. GTA 5 stops at five. GTA 6 goes to six.
Rob Nelson said, quote, "One star is not going to be too difficult to get away from."
That's true. And the important word in that sentence is "one."
But notice what all of these states have in common. None of them is "safe."
Because in GTA 6, the stars going away doesn't mean they've stopped looking.

## CHAPTER 5 — THE HEAT

In GTA 5, escaping was geometry.
Get out of the vision cones on the minimap, wait for the stars to stop flashing, and you're done.
The cops had a cone. You stayed out of it.
In GTA 6, guides report that heat lingers in the area where the crime happened, and cools down over time.
Police search using what they know, your description, clues around the scene, not just your last known position.
So the cone became a whole neighbourhood.
And here's the trap. If you go back, or they spot you again while you still match the description, guides say your old wanted level can snap right back.
You didn't escape. You just paused it.
Even the response is different. Police reportedly don't appear the instant a crime's reported. There's a delay while dispatch sends someone.
Which sounds like a gift, and it is. It's the time you get to make a decision.
Because in GTA 6, running isn't enough anymore.
You have to change.

## CHAPTER 6 — THE ESCAPE

So let's do the fast round. Here's how people escaped in GTA 5.
Number one: the tunnel. Dive in, wait for the cone to pass.
Number two: the respray. Out of sight, into the paint shop, clean.
Number three: just outdrive them.
Number four, in GTA Online: pay Lester, and the cops look the other way.
Every one of those is about the police's eyes. Not one is about who you are.
Now GTA 6. The guides agree on a playbook, and it starts the same way. Break line of sight. Get out of the search area.
But then you look at those icons, and you start taking them away one at a time.
Change your clothes.
Remember: they remember your shirt. So lose the shirt.
Swap the car. If they've flagged your getaway car, a different one stops them matching it.
And this is the new one: split up. If police are looking for a couple, Jason and Lucia going separate ways makes that description a lot harder to match.
That is the whole shift. In GTA 5, you escaped the police. In GTA 6, you escape the description.
Now, the rumour pile.
One guide says Pay 'n' Spray clears your wanted level at any star count. Others don't mention it at all. Treat it as a rumour until you've tested it.
Can you stop a witness before they call it in, like in Red Dead? Not confirmed.
And one guide says there's no cheat that lowers your wanted level.
Which brings us to the one level where none of this playbook works.

## CHAPTER 7 — THE SIXTH STAR

Six stars.
GTA 3, Vice City, San Andreas, GTA 4, they all had a sixth star. GTA 5 took it away.
GTA 6 brings it back, and at six stars, reports say you're pursued across the whole map.
And according to the guides, this is where the playbook stops working. The usual escapes largely don't apply. It ends one of two ways. Busted. Or wasted.
That's from guides, not Rockstar. But think about why it would work that way.
Every system we've talked about is a way of losing information. No witness, nothing to report. Change the shirt, lose an icon. Leave the area, let the heat cool.
At six stars, they don't need a description. They know exactly who you are.

## ENDING

So here's the real difference between the two games.
GTA 5 asked one question: can they see you?
GTA 6 asks a different one: do they know who you are?
In GTA 5, you escaped by getting out of sight.
In GTA 6, you escape by becoming someone they're not looking for.
Because the dispatch, as Rockstar put it, is extremely specific in what it knows.
